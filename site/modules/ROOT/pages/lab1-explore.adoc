= Lab 1: Explore the Knowledge Graph

Use Neo4j Explore to visually navigate and analyze your knowledge graph. You will search for patterns, visualize relationships, and apply graph algorithms to gain insights from the SEC financial data.

TIP: For sign-up and data loading, see xref:lab1-instructions.adoc[Lab 1 Instructions: Set Up Aura and Load the Graph].

== Access the Aura Console

Go to the Neo4j Aura console at https://console.neo4j.io[console.neo4j.io].

== Open Explore

In the left sidebar, click **Explore** under the Tools section.

image::lab1-open-explore.png[Open Explore]

Click **Connect to instance** and select your database instance.

image::lab1-connect-instance.png[Connect Instance]

== Search for Asset Manager Relationships

In the search bar, build a pattern to explore relationships between asset managers, companies, and risk factors:

1. Type `AssetManager`
2. Select the **OWNS** relationship
3. Select **Company**
4. Select the **FACES_RISK** relationship
5. Select **RiskFactor**

This creates the pattern: `AssetManager -- OWNS -> Company -- FACES_RISK -> RiskFactor`

image::lab1-build-pattern.png[Build Pattern]

== Visualize the Knowledge Graph

After executing the search, you will see a visual representation showing:

* **AssetManager nodes** (orange/salmon): Institutional investors like BlackRock, Vanguard
* **Company nodes** (pink): Tech companies with SEC filings
* **RiskFactor nodes** (yellow): Risk categories extracted from filings

The visualization reveals how different asset managers are exposed to various risk factors through the companies they own.

image::lab1-company-graph.png[Company Graph]

=== Navigation Tips

**Zoom and Pan:**

* **Zoom**: Scroll wheel or pinch gesture
* **Pan**: Click and drag the canvas
* **Center**: Double-click on empty space

**Inspect Nodes and Relationships:**

* Click on a node to see its properties
* Click on a relationship to see its type and properties
* Double-click a node to expand and see more connections

== Explore the Competitive Landscape

Clear the current search and build a new pattern:

1. Type `Company`
2. Select the **COMPETES_WITH** relationship
3. Select **Company**

This creates the pattern: `Company -- COMPETES_WITH -> Company`

The visualization shows which filing companies compete with which other companies. You will see Microsoft with the most competitor edges, followed by NVIDIA and Apple. The outer ring of smaller nodes are mentioned companies (Alphabet, Oracle, Samsung, and others) that appear in filings but are not filing companies themselves.

== Apply Degree Centrality

NOTE: This requires Graph Analytics to be enabled on your Aura instance. In the Aura Console, open your instance settings and, under **Graph Analytics**, select **Plugin**. If it is not enabled, you will see a 403 error when applying an algorithm.

With the competitive landscape still on the canvas, open the **Graph Data Science** panel by clicking the connected-nodes icon in the left toolbar.

image::lab1-gds-panel.png[Graph Data Science]

Degree Centrality counts the number of relationships each node has. In this competitive graph, it answers the question: which company has the most competitive connections?

1. Click **Add algorithm**
2. Select **Degree Centrality** from the Algorithm dropdown
3. Click **Apply algorithm** to run the analysis

image::lab1-community-results.png[Degree Centrality Results]

== Size Nodes Based on Scores

After the algorithm completes, click **Size nodes based on scores** to visually represent the centrality: nodes with more competitive connections appear larger.

Microsoft dominates the visualization (32 competitor edges), making it immediately obvious which filing company operates in the most competitive markets. Mentioned companies like Alphabet and Oracle stay small because they only have a single incoming edge.

This ranking is more useful than a raw edge list. Degree Centrality surfaces the most connected companies at a glance, which is a common first step when analyzing a network: find the hubs, then investigate why they are so central.

== Additional Exploration Ideas

Try these patterns to explore more of the knowledge graph:

=== Explore Company Products
----
Company -- OFFERS -> Product
----
Reveals products and services mentioned in filings.

=== Partner Network
----
Company -- PARTNERS_WITH -> Company
----
Shows supply chain and strategic partnerships. NVIDIA's partner list reveals its semiconductor supply chain.

=== Compare Risk Factors
Click on a specific RiskFactor node and expand to see which companies share that risk.
